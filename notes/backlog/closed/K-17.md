# K-17 (full-card cliff check for 1024-thread launches): CLOSED — the cliff does not bite as written; the quantiser's launch gains 4-18 % from the step, the expand's gains nothing, and only the quantiser takes the pad

Item as written (backlog K-17, `notes/backlog/optimisation-backlog.md`): "Expand launches sit on the full-card cliff
wave count at 32 heads: 10-minute check. `r4d_mla_kv_bf16.hip:530` grid (2, 32, ceil(M/16)) x 1024 threads = 2048
waves; rule `r4d_common.h:349-375` (waves in 2017..2048 of a multiple of 2048 run ~5x slower; measured on 256-thread
workgroups); no expand launcher calls `r4d_cliff_rows`. Also on the count: absorb 32 heads M 17..32, `merge_sink`
sliding M = 16 / sparse M = 32, sel_sink 16 slots M = 32." Why: "the stamps do **not** show a 5x expand in sliding
layers ... so the cliff probably does not bite at 1024-thread workgroups; ... A kbench A/B (one padded idle workgroup)
settles it." Verify: "`rad-kbench --timing-only` heads 32, M 1 / 16 / 32, with and without the pad." BI, effort S.

The stamps' guess was half right. Measured on gpu-mini (2x R9700, one card free with the serving stack down, 3297 MHz
after warm-up), the item's launch shapes pay **nothing** near the rule's window — neither the 1024-thread quantiser's
`grid (2, 32, ceil(M/16))` at 2048 waves, nor the 128-thread plain expand's `16 x 32` at 2048 — while a third shape on
the same launches, the quantiser at 32 heads, gains a small but consistent **4-18 %** from stepping the head axis past
the multiple. The 5x of `r4d_common.h:349-375` is a 256-thread-workgroup effect and does not transfer to these
workgroup sizes at full-card wave counts. This branch takes the pad for the quantiser's launcher alone, where the gain
is measured; the plain expand's head axis stays unstepped, with a comment at its launch site.

## The A/B

Both arms are `build-lane` (`-DRAD_WITH_HIP=ON -DRAD_GPU_TARGETS=gfx1201`) builds of the same tree, `libr4d.so` swapped
between them (base `a074cea6`, pad `e7753320`); `rad-kbench --home ... --bench --timing-only --max-cases 1 --op
mla_project_kv --op mla_expand_gate --fixture data/kernels_hy4.rkb --no-ref`, one session per arm, per-case medians of
14-20 batches, interval half-widths at most +-4.3 %. The pad arm builds the guards and both `r4d_cliff_rows` steps;
before the revert below it stepped **every** 32-head row that lands on a multiple: the quantiser's (2, 32, zt) grids
(zt = 1 -> 2048 waves, 2 -> 4096, 4 -> 8192) to 33 heads, the plain expand's and absorb's (16, 32, groups) and
(8, 32, groups) grids to 33 heads wherever `32 x 16 x 4 x groups` (or `x 8 x ...`) is a multiple of 2048, which it is
for every groups < 32. The card-less `tests/r4d_emu/test_mla_kv.cpp` drives the real expand launchers and passed on the
pad build, and a full `rad-kbench` check pass (no `--timing-only`) ran in the same window on both arms: **108/108
cases, every byte identical on the pad build**, including all 32-head quantiser rows — the quantiser is not in the
emulator, so that check is its byte evidence.

Medians, base vs padded (base/pad > 1 means the pad is faster):

the A/B's control that the two sessions ran under the same clock state: they sit at 0.98-1.02 while every stepped
quantiser row sits at 1.04-1.18, so the quantiser's gain is the step, not the session.

## What this says

- **The 5x cliff is a 256-thread-workgroup effect.** The rule's own numbers (27.6 vs 5.2 us at 253-256 vs 252/257
  workgroups) were measured with 256-thread workgroups. At 128-thread workgroups the full-card count costs nothing
  (expand h32 M=1: 16.92 us unstepped, 17.00 stepped), and at 1024-thread the count costs 4-18 %, not 5x. A launch's
  exposure to the window is a property of the workgroup shape as much as of the wave count; `r4d_cliff_rows` applied
  blindly to a non-256-thread grid buys a step with nothing to recover, as the expand rows show.
- **The quantiser's gain is real and the merged pad is only this half.** At 32 heads — every sliding layer's decode
  shape, fused `RADIANCE_HY4_FUSE_EXPAND_QUANT` path — one launch goes from 24.5 us to 22.9 us at M = 1, about
  1.7 us x 30 sliding layers = ~0.05 ms a token. The step is the existing `r4d_cliff_rows`; the extra head's workgroup
  returns at the top of `kvq_body`, and the chained wrapper still runs `r4d_chain_done()` for it, which the engine
  expects since it counts blocks from the issued packet grid (`aql.cpp:1412`). Bytes are identical (check arm, above).
- **The audit's T-1 companions are expected nulls.** `merge_sink` sliding M = 16 and `sel_sink` 16 slots M = 32 ride
  128-thread workgroups at the 2048 count — the shape this A/B measured at 0.995-1.006 — and absorb at 32 heads
  M 17..32 was in the table above at 0.99. None is worth a padded launch; if anyone re-tests one, the finding here is
  the prior.
- **One gap stays open:** the fixture has no quantiser rows at 32 heads M = 16 or 32, so the stepped region between
  the M = 1 (1.07) and M = 17 (1.05) rows is interpolated, not measured. Nothing suggests the sign changes there;
  rows 16/32 at h32 belong in a future fixture pass.
